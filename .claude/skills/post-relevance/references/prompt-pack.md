# Prompt pack: the eight-pass relevance workflow

Copy-paste ready, in order. Keep **one conversation** for all eight passes,
because every prompt builds on the previous result.

Normalised from the original notes: em dashes removed, US spellings corrected,
and a voice preamble added so the output comes back usable whichever model runs
it. Never ask any of these to "make this viral".

---

## Paste this first, once, at the top of the conversation

> Before we start, these rules apply to everything you write for me from here
> on, in every pass.
>
> Use UK and New Zealand spelling. Realise, colour, favourite, behaviour,
> programme, analyse, organise, prioritise.
>
> Never use em dashes or en dashes. Use commas, full stops, ellipses, colons or
> separate sentences instead.
>
> Always use contractions. Can't, don't, won't, isn't, you've, it's.
>
> The voice is conversational, direct, warm and a little rebellious. Like a
> coach talking to a close friend, not a corporate copywriter. Spoken word
> rather than written prose. Sentence fragments are fine. Run-ons are fine.
> Trailing thoughts with ellipses are fine. Short paragraphs. Vary the sentence
> length. Multiple short questions in a row are good when they build rhythm.
>
> Avoid: stiff transitions like "furthermore" and "moreover", therapist-speak
> like "research shows" and "studies suggest", coaching clichés like "queen
> energy" and "boss babe", performative empathy like "I see you", long
> paragraphs, over-polished prose.
>
> Do not invent statistics, sources, client stories or product features. If a
> claim needs a source I do not have, flag it instead of inventing one.
>
> Defend every creative decision you make. If I ask why you chose something, I
> want the reasoning, not a new version.

## Pass 1: confirm the idea matters, the "why should I care?" test

> Act as a brutally honest content strategist.
>
> Read my audience, promised result and draft.
>
> Answer these questions:
>
> 1. Why would someone stop scrolling for this?
> 2. What specific problem or desire does it connect to?
> 3. Is the promised result clear within three seconds?
> 4. Which sentence feels generic, predictable or unnecessary?
> 5. What is missing that would make the post immediately useful?
> 6. What part sounds impressive but provides no value?
> 7. Would my target reader save, share or comment? Explain why.
>
> Do not rewrite the post yet.
>
> Give it a relevance score from 1 to 10.
>
> If the score is below 8, identify the one strategic change that would improve
> it most.

**Gate:** below 8, make that one change and re-score. Do not move on until the
score is 8 or higher.

---

## Pass 2: find the strongest hook, the stress test

> Generate 15 stronger hooks for this exact post.
>
> Create:
>
> - 3 curiosity hooks
> - 3 result-driven hooks
> - 3 contrarian hooks
> - 3 personal-experience hooks
> - 3 urgent or news-style hooks
>
> Rules:
>
> - Each hook must be understandable without context.
> - Use specific outcomes instead of vague claims.
> - Do not use fake urgency or unsupported statistics.
> - Avoid common AI phrases.
> - Keep every hook short enough to read in three seconds.
> - Preserve the original promise of the post.
> - Do not reveal all the value in the hook.
>
> Then rank the best five based on:
>
> 1. Scroll-stopping power
> 2. Clarity
> 3. Credibility
> 4. Curiosity
> 5. Relevance to my audience
>
> Choose one final hook and explain why it should outperform the original.

**Gate:** exactly one hook carries forward.

---

## Pass 3: remove confusion

> Rewrite the post so nobody has to read any sentence twice.
>
> Use the winning hook from the previous step.
>
> For every sentence, check:
>
> - Does it introduce one clear idea?
> - Does the next sentence follow naturally?
> - Can it be shortened without losing meaning?
> - Does it contain jargon that should be simplified?
> - Does it repeat something already said?
> - Does it make a claim that needs context or proof?
>
> Rewrite at an eighth-grade reading level.
>
> Keep the tone confident and conversational.
>
> Use short paragraphs with natural rhythm.
>
> Do not make the post longer.
>
> Return:
>
> 1. The improved version
> 2. Every sentence you removed
> 3. Why each removal improved the post.

**Gate:** the post must not be longer than it was. Simplifying the language is
not the same as flattening the rhythm, so keep the fragments and the ellipses.

---

## Pass 4: increase the value

> The post is clear, but now make the content genuinely worth saving.
>
> Identify the most important promise in the post.
>
> Add only information that helps the reader achieve that promise:
>
> - A practical framework
> - Exact steps
> - A copy-and-paste template
> - A realistic example
> - A mistake to avoid
> - A verification or safety step
> - A clear first action
>
> Remove anything that exists only to make the post look longer.
>
> Organise the value into numbered comment sections.
>
> Each section must:
>
> 1. Have a short uppercase header.
> 2. Deliver a complete takeaway.
> 3. Be understandable without reading later comments.
> 4. End with the next logical action.
> 5. Avoid repeating previous sections.
>
> Prioritise usefulness over quantity.

**Gate:** four strong sections beat five with one thin one.

---

## Pass 5: make the language human, the stop-sounding-like-AI edit

> Edit this post like an experienced human creator, not a corporate
> copywriter.
>
> Remove or replace:
>
> - Empty hype
> - Predictable transitions
> - Unnecessary adjectives
> - Repetitive sentence patterns
> - Fake personal stories
> - "It's not X, it's Y"
> - "In today's fast-paced world"
> - "Game-changer"
> - "Revolutionary"
> - "Unlock"
> - "Dive into"
> - Anything that sounds polished but unnatural
>
> Keep the language direct, specific and conversational.
>
> Do not add slang that I would not naturally use.
>
> Preserve the hook, facts and useful details.
>
> Show me:
>
> 1. The humanised version
> 2. The five biggest AI-writing patterns you removed.

**Exception to paste with it:** "Unlock" stays when it names one of my actual
offers, the Unlock Your Desire Challenge or the 5 Keys to Unlock Your Desire
webinar. Strip it only when it is being used as a lazy verb.

---

## Pass 6: add reasons to save, share and comment

> Analyse the post for saveability and shareability.
>
> SAVEABILITY:
> What would make someone need this again next week?
>
> SHAREABILITY:
> Who would they send it to, and why?
>
> COMMENTABILITY:
> What experience, choice or result could they easily discuss?
>
> Improve the post by adding:
>
> - One reusable resource
> - One concrete example
> - One line worth sharing
> - One low-effort but meaningful question
>
> Do not use generic questions such as:
>
> "What do you think?"
> "Do you agree?"
> "Which one is your favourite?"
>
> Create five specific engagement questions.
>
> Select the one most connected to the post's value.

**Voice note to paste with it:** my engagement questions usually take one of
these shapes. `Comment 'connection'` and similar one-word comment prompts.
`Say with me "I choose me"`. `Ask your body "how are you doing, darling?"`.
Or a direct invitation like `Are you ready?`.

---

## Pass 7: build the publishing package

> Turn everything we created into a final publishing package.
>
> Return it in this exact order:
>
> A. FINAL CAPTION
> - Strong hook
> - Maximum three short paragraphs
> - Do not reveal all the value
> - End by directing readers to the comments
>
> B. PINNED COMMENTS
> - Divide the guide into logical numbered comments
> - Use uppercase mini-headlines
> - Include every prompt, step, example and useful link
> - Never place one tiny tip in an entire comment
> - Keep each comment valuable on its own
>
> C. VISUAL TITLE
> - Give me five title options
> - Maximum eight words each
> - Make them readable on mobile
>
> D. VISUAL DIRECTION
> - Square 1:1
> - One obvious visual story
> - Clean composition
> - No unnecessary objects
> - Leave space for the title
>
> E. FACT CHECK
> List every claim, number, product feature or link that requires verification
> before publishing.
>
> Do not invent sources or features.

---

## Pass 8: red-team the final result

> You are the editor responsible for rejecting weak content.
>
> Try to stop me from publishing this post.
>
> Find:
>
> - The weakest sentence
> - The least believable claim
> - The section people may skip
> - The part competitors have already repeated
> - Any promise the comments fail to deliver
> - Any reason the visual and caption feel disconnected
> - Any factual claim requiring verification
> - Any phrase that could reduce trust
>
> Score the post from 1 to 10 for:
>
> Hook
> Clarity
> Originality
> Usefulness
> Credibility
> Saveability
> Shareability
> Comment potential
>
> Do not approve it unless every score is at least 8.
>
> If anything scores lower, fix only that weakness and return the final version.

**Gate:** all eight scores at 8 or higher. Fix only the named weakness, then
re-emit the affected part of the package and re-score. If it hands back eight
9s on the first look without finding anything, it is not doing the job. Run it
again and tell it to be harsher.

