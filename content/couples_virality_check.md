# Virality Check: Couples Campaign

Every piece in `couples_campaign.md` and `couples_newsletters.md` was run through the Click-Hold-Share test, then checked against the language rules (`content/style_guide.md` plus the voice rules in the website-build skill). Weak pieces were rewritten in place.

This is a judgement check, not a prediction from your analytics. Once these go live, your own numbers beat this table.

## The test

- **Click:** does the first line stop the scroll? Would she open it?
- **Hold:** does she get to the end? Short lines, a turn, a payoff.
- **Share:** would she send it to her partner or a friend, or tag someone?

Plus one gate for this campaign: **does it end by pointing at the discovery session?**

## What failed and what changed

### The big pattern: openers that start with a label
"EFT doesn't...", "Polarity is...", "Deida talks about..." are fine for someone who already knows the words. A woman scrolling at 11pm doesn't. Those openers now start with what she's feeling, and the label comes second.

### Share prompts added
Nothing in a relationship niche spreads like "send this to your partner". It's the post doing the hard conversation for her. Added to: EFT 2, EFT 5 ("Tag them. Gently."), AVOID 2, AVOID 6, POLARITY 1, REEL 2.

### Discovery session gate
Several posts ended on a question with no next step, and Reel 3 asked for a different word (BRAKES). Every post, reel, the video and every email now ends with the discovery session. Questions that drive comments stay, with the CTA after them.

### Language rules: what I'd got wrong
- **Too poetic** (your rule: would a woman in her fifties say this to a friend over coffee?):
  - "one problem wearing two outfits" became "the same problem" (video script, Email 4)
  - "decorating the house while the foundations are cracking" became "just putting a plaster on it"
  - "love that's been left at the door too long" became "a lonely person asking for more of you"
  - "Your sway. Your hunger." became "Your cheek. The way you used to dance in the kitchen."
  - Email 2 subject "The dance nobody chose" became "Why you keep having the same fight"
- **Invented proof** (your rule: never invent stats, stories or client results):
  - Reel 6 was a made-up client story with "shared with permission" on it. It's now general advice, with a note to swap in a story only if you have written consent.
  - Email 4 said you'd lain in the lonely bed yourself. I don't know that, so it now talks about the women you kept meeting. Put your own story back in if it's true.
  - Email 5 ("most couples feel relief in the first few sessions") and Email 8 ("I've watched it happen over and over") made claims about results. Both are softened.
  - "Most therapists do one or the other" became "Lots of therapists..."
  - EFT 1 (an invented client quote), Reel 5 ("I used to hand couples tools"), POLARITY 7 ("this idea changed my marriage"), POLARITY 6 ("most couples I see") and AVOID 10 ("I'm working with 10 couples right now", which isn't true yet) are all reworded so they don't claim things about you or your practice.
- **Clean already:** no dashes, UK spelling, contractions, no "research shows", no "I see you", no hype. The word "real" doesn't appear anywhere.

## Round 2: Vex and Leo checks, plus an AI-pattern sweep

### Vex (hooks): every opener must use at least one of four hooks
- **Open loop:** a question or tension she has to stay to resolve.
- **Pattern interrupt:** something unexpected or a bit cheeky.
- **Identity:** she feels personally called out.
- **Stakes:** what she loses if she scrolls past.

### Leo (long video): open loops and a re-hook every 60 to 90 seconds
The 5 minute script had a strong open loop at the start, but it went nearly two minutes without a re-hook. Two were added:
- At the start: "And at the end I'll give you three things you can try tonight." This plants a loop that pays off at 3:40.
- At about 1:40: "Stay with me, because this is where it gets interesting." This comes just before the sexual cycle section.

The existing beats at 2:50 ("These two cycles are married to each other") and 3:40 ("So what actually works?") already do the job.
