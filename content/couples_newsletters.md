## How StoryBrand runs through these

- **The hero is her (and her partner).** Not you. It's her story.
- **The problem has three layers:**
  - Outside: we hardly have sex, and when we do it's tense.
  - Inside: I feel lonely, unwanted, like I'm failing.
  - Bigger truth: two people who love each other shouldn't have to live like flatmates.
- **You're the guide.** You get it (empathy) and you've done the training (authority).
- **The plan is 3 simple steps:** book a discovery session, see your cycle, come back to each other.
- **The call to action is always the same:** book your discovery session.
- **What's at stake:** years more of polite distance.
- **What success looks like:** reaching for each other in the kitchen again, and wanting to.

### Email 2 (Wednesday)

**Subject:** Why you keep having the same fight
**Preview:** One reaches. One retreats. Round and round.

Hi [First name],

I want to show you the dance so many couples get stuck in.

In Emotionally Focused Therapy (the couples work I'm trained in) we call it the cycle. It goes like this.

One of you reaches. You ask for time together. You bring up the sex thing again. You say "you never..." because you're hurting and you don't know how else to say it.

The other one retreats. Goes quiet. Goes to their phone. Says "I'm tired". Hopes it'll blow over.

So the reacher reaches harder. And the retreater retreats further.

Underneath the reaching is a question: "Do I matter to you?"
Underneath the retreating is a fear: "I keep getting it wrong."

Two people, both scared of losing each other, protecting the relationship in opposite ways.

Neither of you is the bad guy. The cycle is.

And when you stop fighting each other and start fighting the cycle together? Everything changes.

That's what we start mapping in a discovery session. You'll leave knowing exactly what your dance looks like and what to do about it.

### Email 6 (Friday)

**Subject:** Try this tonight (5 minutes)
**Preview:** A tiny experiment for two

Hi [First name],

I want to give you something you can do tonight. No therapy needed.

When your partner walks in the door, stop what you're doing.

Look at them. Properly. Phone down.

Say "Hi. I'm glad you're home."

Hold a hug for 20 seconds. Long enough for your bodies to relax. With no expectation that it goes anywhere else.

That's all.

Why does this work? Because presence is the most attractive thing you can offer someone. David Deida writes about this beautifully. Full attention says "you matter" louder than any words.

And when a hug no longer comes with a question attached, the partner who's been avoiding touch can finally breathe.

Try it for a week. Then hit reply and tell me what happened.

### Bonus Email 2 (Wednesday)

**Subject:** You can say no and still love him\
**Preview:** The option nobody taught us

Hi [First name],

Most of us grew up with two ways to handle a no.

One: say yes anyway. Keep the peace. Then resent it for a week.

Two: say no and shut the whole house down. The door, the heart, the body, all of it.

So we swing between them. Years of yes, yes, yes... and then one day we're so done that every no comes out like a slammed door.

There's a third way. You keep your no, and you keep your heart open while you say it.

It sounds like this.

"Not tonight. And I love you. Come here, let me hold you."

"I can't do dinner with your parents this weekend. I know it matters to you, so let's find another time."

"It hurts when you're on your phone at dinner. I want you here with me."

Your no matters. It tells your partner where you end and they begin. And a no that comes with warmth is so much easier to hear. They don't have to defend themselves. They can come closer instead.

Your no looks after you. Your open heart looks after the two of you. You don't have to choose.

Try one this week. Just one. Say your no, and leave your heart in the room while you do it.

If you two keep getting stuck between giving in and shutting down, that's exactly what I help couples with. Start with a free discovery session.

### Bonus Email 3 (Friday)

**Subject:** How to stay open when you want to shut down\
**Preview:** 3 steps for the moment you feel yourself go cold

Hi [First name],

This week we've talked about the moment you shut down, and about saying no without closing your heart.

Today is about the moment itself. When you feel yourself going cold, try these three steps.

**1. Notice it.**\
Feel where your body tightens. Name it to yourself. "I'm closing."

**2. Breathe and soften.**\
One slow breath into your belly. Let your chest soften, just a little. You're not giving in. You're staying in the room.

**3. Say what's going on, with your heart still open.**\
What hurts, and what you want. "I miss you when you're on your phone. I want some time with you tonight."

You'll close again. Probably tomorrow. That's okay. Staying open is something you practise, and every time you close, you can open again.

It's a lot easier when you're not the only one doing it.

In couples sessions we practise this together, slowly, so neither of you is left holding the door open alone. You learn to catch the moment you both start closing, and to turn back towards each other instead.
