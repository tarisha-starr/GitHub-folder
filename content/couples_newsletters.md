# 9 Newsletters: The Discovery Session Sequence

Three a week for three weeks: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.
Every email has one job: get them to book a free discovery session.

Swap **[BOOK YOUR DISCOVERY SESSION]** for your booking link, as a button and as a plain text link.

## How StoryBrand runs through these

- **The hero is her (and her partner).** Not you. She's the one on the journey.
- **The problem has three layers:**
  - Outside: we hardly have sex, and when we do it's tense.
  - Inside: I feel lonely, unwanted, like I'm failing.
  - Bigger truth: two people who love each other shouldn't have to live like flatmates.
- **You're the guide.** You get it (empathy) and you've done the training (authority).
- **The plan is 3 simple steps:** book a discovery session, see your cycle, come back to each other.
- **The call to action is always the same:** book your discovery session.
- **What's at stake:** years more of polite distance.
- **What success looks like:** reaching for each other in the kitchen again, and wanting to.

### Email 2 (Wednesday)

**Subject:** Why you keep having the same fight
**Preview:** One reaches. One retreats. Round and round.

Hi [First name],

Let's talk about the dance.

In Emotionally Focused Therapy (the couples work I'm trained in) we call it the cycle. It goes like this.

One of you reaches. You ask for time together. You bring up the sex thing again. You say "you never..." because you're hurting and you don't know how else to say it.

The other one retreats. Goes quiet. Goes to their phone. Says "I'm tired". Hopes it'll blow over.

So the reacher reaches harder. And the retreater retreats further.

Here's what I want you to know.

Underneath the reaching is a question: "Do I matter to you?"
Underneath the retreating is a fear: "I keep getting it wrong."

Two people, both scared of losing each other, protecting the relationship in opposite ways.

Nobody's the villain. The cycle is the villain.

And when you stop fighting each other and start fighting the cycle together? Everything changes.

That's what we start mapping in a discovery session. You'll leave knowing exactly what your dance looks like and what to do about it.

### Email 6 (Friday)

**Subject:** Try this tonight (5 minutes)
**Preview:** A tiny experiment for two

Hi [First name],

I want to give you something you can do tonight. No therapy needed.

When your partner walks in the door, stop what you're doing.

Look at them. Properly. Phone down.

Say "Hi. I'm glad you're home."

Hold a hug for 20 seconds. Long enough for your bodies to relax. With no expectation that it goes anywhere else.

That's all.

Why does this work? Because presence is the most attractive thing you can offer someone. David Deida writes about this beautifully. Full attention says "you matter" louder than any words.

And when a hug no longer comes with a question attached, the partner who's been avoiding touch can finally breathe.

Try it for a week. Then hit reply and tell me what happened.

And if you notice how much you've been missing each other? That's your cue. Let's talk about getting you two back properly.
