# SESSION 1: The Open Heart (120 min)

**Intention:** she learns to stay with love and keep her heart open, to tell the
difference between vulnerable and emotional, and to express even anger from a connected
heart, then to open her whole body to love.

## Part 1: Arrive & ground (0:00-0:12)

Welcome, candle, agreements (confidential; we witness, we don't fix; you can always pass;
no such thing as too much). Soften them in: feet on the floor, soften the jaw, shoulders,
belly, three slow breaths. One-word go-round: how are you arriving?

## Part 2: Polarity, the three arts, the teachings (0:12-0:27)

> Attraction isn't random. It lives in polarity, the attraction between love and presence.

**What is polarity?** Polarity is the attraction between love and presence, between the
feminine and the masculine. The pull comes from their difference, and the greater the
difference, the stronger the charge.

**Masculine energy:** the masculine is presence, a steady, grounded, focused energy that
holds and gives structure, the banks of the river. It lives in all of us, and here it is
his gift.

**Feminine energy:** the feminine is love in motion, radiant and flowing and alive with
feeling. She is the river. This energy lives in all of us too, and here it is her gift.

Lay out the three arts (the map for both sessions):
1. **Stay with love**, with an open heart.
2. **Amplify your expression.**
3. **Amplify polarity.**

Introduce Deida and his two books (show the book slide). Name the essence-not-gender frame.

## Part 3: Art 1: Stay with love (0:27-0:40)

> The first art, and the ground of all of it: stay with love. Keep your heart open, even
> when it's hard. Love is openness. The moment you close, you contract, and the attraction
> goes flat.

**Vulnerable vs emotional** (your teaching, the heart of this session)
> Here's the distinction that changes everything. When you're connected to your heart,
> you're vulnerable, and you're still loving, even in your anger. When you're disconnected
> from your heart, you become emotional, and it lands as an attack. And a partner has every
> right to protect himself from an attack. That's not him failing to love you. That's him
> defending.

**Anger from the heart**
> So we don't make anger wrong. You can feel the full fire of anger and still stay
> connected. Same fire, open heart. That's vulnerability, not attack. That's the skill.

## Part 4: ★ Group exercise: Anger from the heart (0:40-1:05)

Run this with the whole group.

1. **Demo.** Ask for a volunteer. Have her name something she feels angry about.
   - First, let her say it the **disconnected** way, as blame, from a closed heart. The
     group feels how it lands as an attack.
   - Then guide her: hand on her heart, say the same thing **connected**, "I feel angry,
     and I still love you. What I need is..." The group feels the difference.
2. **Whole-group practice.** Everyone brings something they're angry about, hand on heart,
   and speaks it aloud (or silently) from the connected place, staying with love.
3. **Share.** A few voices: what changed when you kept your heart open?

**Facilitator note:** keep it safe and warm. The point isn't to perform anger, it's to feel
that you can be angry *and* loving at once.

## Break (1:05-1:12)

## Part 5: Open to love in the whole body (1:12-1:17)

> Love isn't only in the heart. We can open the heart and the whole body, more and more.
> There's no limit to how much love you can let in, and let move through you.

## Part 6: ★ Group practice: Open your heart & whole body (1:17-1:35)

Guide the whole group, slowly, eyes closed:

> Hand on your heart, hand on your belly. Breathe. Feel love in your chest, a warmth, a
> softness. Now let it spread, down through your belly, your hips, your legs, out through
> your arms, up through your throat and face. Your whole body, open to love. With each
> breath, open a little more. There's no limit. Let love keep filling you and moving
> through you.

Let them stay in it for several minutes. Then a gentle return and a word or two on what
they noticed.

## Part 7: Devotion (1:35-1:50)

The deepest layer of staying with love.

> **What devotion is:** devotion is the feminine's deepest practice. It's opening your
> heart and offering your love, again and again, as a gift. Not love you earn, and not love
> you hold back to stay safe. Love you give, freely, to your partner, to life, to the
> divine. The open heart from Art 1 becomes a way of living when you make it devotional.

**The practice of devotion:**
- Open your heart as an offering, every day, not only when you feel like it.
- Give your love freely. Radiate it. Don't ration it or make him earn it.
- Let your relationship be a doorway to something greater than the two of you.
- When you feel yourself wanting to close or protect, choose to open to love instead. That
  choice, made again and again, is devotion.

Close with a minute of practice: hand on heart, and silently, "I choose to stay open. I
offer my love." Invite a word on what they felt.

**Reflect:** When, and how, are you withholding your love?

## Part 8: Integration & take-home (1:50-2:00)

**Take-home:**
> This week: when anger rises, keep one hand on your heart. Feel it, speak it, stay
> connected. Each day, open your whole body to love a little more. And practise devotion,
> choosing to open and offer your love, especially when you'd rather close.

Closing round, one line each: "The way I'll keep my heart open this week is..."

# SESSION 2: Amplifying Expression & Polarity (120 min)

**Intention:** she learns to amplify her expression so her partner can feel her, to
encourage expression in another woman (Deida's coaching method), and to amplify polarity,
amplifying the feminine so her partner is drawn into his masculine presence.

| Time | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:12 | **Arrive & recap** | Re-ground |
| 0:12-0:27 | **Art 2: Amplify your expression** | Show, don't hint |
| 0:27-0:37 | **How to encourage her** | Deida's feedback method |
| 0:37-0:42 | **Reflect** | |
| 0:42-1:08 | **★ Paired exercise: Amplify your expression** | With encouraging feedback |
| 1:08-1:12 | **Break** | |
| 1:12-1:27 | **Art 3: Amplify polarity** | The feminine pole; draw him into presence; river & banks |
| 1:27-1:32 | **Reflect** | |
| 1:32-1:55 | **★ Paired exercise: Amplifying polarity** | |
| 1:55-2:00 | **The three arts together & close** | |

## Part 1: Arrive & recap (0:00-0:12)

Soften them in. Recap Session 1: love is openness; stay with your heart; vulnerable, not
emotional. Go-round: one way I kept my heart open this week.

## Part 2: Art 2: Amplify your expression (0:12-0:27)

> Your inner world, your feeling, your wanting, your delight, only reaches him when you
> show it. He can't feel what you keep hidden or only hint at. So let it move through your
> body, your face, your voice, your movement, so he can actually feel you and understand you.
>
> Most of us learned to hold it in, to be contained, to hint and hope he'll notice. Tonight
> we practise the opposite: letting it out, big, through the body.

## Part 3: How to encourage her (0:27-0:37)

This is Deida's way of teaching expression, through an encouraging witness.

> In the exercise, one of you expresses, and the other encourages. The witness's whole job
> is to draw her out and celebrate her, never to critique.
>
> You encourage more: "Relax here a little more." "Give me more expression." And you affirm
> when it lands, when you can actually feel her: "Yes, I can really feel you."
>
> That affirmation is the gift. It tells her body it's safe to show more, and she opens.

## Part 4: Reflect (0:37-0:42)

Pose: Where do I hold my expression in? What does my body do when I let it show?

## Part 5: ★ Paired exercise: Amplify your expression (0:42-1:08)

In pairs (breakout rooms on Zoom). Partner A and B.

1. **Partner A expresses** a feeling, fully, through face, voice and body. Start where she
   is, then let it grow.
2. **Partner B encourages**, in the Deida style: "relax here a little more," "give me more
   expression," and affirms when she feels her: "yes, I can really feel you." No critique.
3. **Let A grow bigger** with the encouragement. Stay with it a few minutes.
4. **Swap.**

**Main-room share:** what happened when someone encouraged you to show more? What did it
feel like to be truly felt?

## Break (1:08-1:12)

## Part 6: Art 3: Amplify polarity (1:12-1:27)

This is the subtle one. Teach it carefully.

> Polarity is the charge between the poles. To amplify it, you amplify the feminine. Not by
> becoming the strong one, presence is his masculine gift, not yours to supply. You amplify
> the feminine, more emotion, more expression through your body, and that draws him into his
> presence.

**Draw him into his presence**
> So when he drops, when he goes flat, or sad, or gives up, you don't shrink, and you don't
> fix, and you don't go strong and structured yourself. You go further into the feeling, not
> away from it. If he's helpless, you become even more helpless. You bring more emotion, more
> expression, through your body, from an open heart. Your amplified feminine calls him into
> his presence, the strong ground for you.

**Be the river** (the key metaphor)
> Think of a river and its banks. A river needs banks to hold it. If you hold the banks
> yourself, if you stay structured, controlled, strong, there's nothing for him to hold, and
> he has no reason to step into his presence. But become the river, softer, more flowing,
> less structured, and he has to become the banks. He has to become the structure and the
> presence for you.

**Keep it heart-connected:** this is amplifying the feminine *from an open heart*, Art 1
still holds. It's not spilling emotion as an attack. It's full feeling, open heart.

## Part 7: Reflect (1:27-1:32)

Pose: How do I usually respond when he drops, do I shrink, fix, or go strong myself? What
would it be like to amplify the feeling, and soften like the river, instead?

## Part 8: ★ Paired exercise: Amplifying polarity (1:32-1:55)

In pairs. (Both play the feminine role in turn, this is about the move, not gender.)

1. **One partner drops** into a low, flat, emotional state, sad, helpless, giving up.
2. **The other** doesn't shrink, fix, or go strong. She **amplifies the same feeling**, more
   emotion, more expression through the body, and **softens, becomes the river**, less
   structured.
3. **Notice** how the amplified, softened feminine calls the other to steady, to bring
   presence, to become the banks.
4. **Swap roles.**

**Main-room share:** what did you feel when your partner softened and amplified instead of
toughening up? What happened to the charge between you?

**Facilitator note:** some women will want to "hold it together" and provide the structure,
that's the habit we're unwinding. Encourage softening and amplifying, gently.

## Part 9: The three arts together & close (1:55-2:00)

> Three arts together. Stay with love and keep your heart open. Let your expression move
> through your body so he can feel you. And amplify polarity by softening into more feeling
> and more expression, like the river, so he rises into his presence.

**Take-home:**
> Show one feeling fully through your body each day. When he drops, don't shrink, amplify
> the feeling and soften like the river. And practise opening your heart and sharing your
> love, especially when you want to close down.

Closing round, one line each: "The art I'm taking home is..."

## Facilitator notes (both sessions)

- **Love is the ground.** Every art rests on the open heart. Amplified emotion from a closed
  heart is an attack, not polarity, so keep bringing it back to Art 1.
- **Do the exercises.** The teaching only sets them up; the group and paired practices are
  the training.
- **Group vs paired:** Session 1's exercises are whole-group (anger demo + heart-and-body
  opening). Session 2's are paired, and the expression one uses the encouraging-feedback
  method.
- **On Zoom:** run the paired exercises in breakout rooms; broadcast the prompt before
  opening; bring everyone back for the main-room share. In person, use live pairs.
- **Essence, not gender:** frame masculine and feminine as energies, so the work includes
  every woman and every relationship.
- **Scope:** this is embodiment and relationship practice, not therapy. If something big
  surfaces, offer one-to-one support.
