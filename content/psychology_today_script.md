# Emotional and Sexual Cycles: Psychology Today Video and Facebook Ads

One master video (about 4 minutes) for your Psychology Today profile, a 60 second cut for Facebook and Instagram ads, and 3 sets of ad copy. Everything points to the free discovery session.

---

## 1. Master script (about 4 minutes)

**Title options:**
- The Two Cycles That Pull Couples Apart
- Why One of You Wants Sex and the Other Pulls Away
- It's Not Your Libido. It's Your Cycle.

**Length:** about 600 words, roughly 4 minutes at a relaxed pace.

**[HOOK, 0:00]**

One of you wants more sex. The other one keeps finding reasons not to.

And somehow you've ended up fighting about the dishes, the phone, the in-laws... everything except the thing that hurts.

If that's your house, you haven't fallen out of love. You're caught in a cycle. Two of them, in fact.

By the end of this video you'll know how to spot both, and three things that help.

**[INTRO, 0:25]**

I'm Tarisha. I'm a relationship and sex therapist in Auckland, trained in Emotionally Focused Therapy and sex therapy.

I work with couples on the heart stuff and the bedroom stuff together, because they're connected.

**[CYCLE ONE: DURING THE DAY, 0:40]**

The first cycle happens during the day.

One partner reaches. They want more time, more closeness, more conversation. When they don't get it, the reaching turns into criticism. "You never..." "Why don't you..."

The other partner pulls back. They go quiet, go to the shed, go to their phone. They're trying to stop things getting worse.

Underneath, both of them are scared. The one reaching is asking, "Do I matter to you?" The one pulling back is thinking, "I can't get this right."

The more one reaches, the more the other retreats. The more one retreats, the harder the other reaches.

**[CYCLE TWO: AT NIGHT, 1:40]**

Now watch the same dance move into the bedroom.

The partner with more desire initiates. The other one feels pressure, and their body puts the brakes on. "I'm tired." "Not tonight."

So they start avoiding anything that might lead to sex. Fewer hugs. Going to bed at different times.

The partner who initiates feels rejected. So they either push harder, or stop trying altogether.

Two lonely people, in the same bed.

**[HOW THEY FEED EACH OTHER, 2:30]**

These two cycles feed each other.

When you feel disconnected during the day, it's hard for your body to open at night. For most people, desire needs safety first.

And when sex keeps going wrong, you feel more distant during the day. More tense. More careful with each other.

That's why date nights and new lingerie don't usually fix it. They don't touch the cycle.

**[WHAT HELPS, 3:10]**

Three things help.

First, name the cycle together. "We're doing the thing again." The cycle becomes the problem, not your partner.

Second, share what's underneath. The fear, not the complaint. "I miss you." "I freeze because I'm scared of letting you down."

Third, take the pressure off sex for a while. Touch that doesn't have to go anywhere. Pleasure over performance. Orgasm isn't the goal. Connection is.

When the pressure comes off and people feel safe, desire often starts to come back.

**[INVITATION, 3:50]**

This is the work I do with couples. EFT for the emotional cycle, sex therapy for the bedroom one, together.

If you saw the two of you in this, get in touch through this page and book a free discovery session. We'll talk about what's happening for you both, and whether I'm the right fit. Auckland or online.

You two don't have to stay stuck.

**[END, about 4:10]**

**Filming notes:**
- Film in one spot, eye level, natural light. Psychology Today viewers are choosing a therapist, so calm and warm beats fancy.
- Add captions. Most people watch with the sound off, and it helps people with hearing loss.
- On screen at the start: your name, "Relationship and Sex Therapist, Auckland and online".
- On screen at the end: couplestherapy.co.nz and tarisha@couplestherapy.co.nz.

## 2. Facebook and Instagram ad cut (60 seconds)

Meta's ad rules are strict about two things here: sexual wording, and lines that suggest the viewer has a personal problem ("Is your sex life dead?"). This cut talks about couples in general and says "intimacy" instead of "sex" in the hook, which keeps it safer. Meta's rules change, so check its Advertising Standards before you run it.

**On-screen hook:** *The 2 cycles that pull couples apart*

**Script (about 150 words):**

"There are two cycles that pull couples apart. One happens during the day. One happens at night.

During the day, one partner reaches for closeness and the other pulls back. The more one reaches, the more the other retreats.

At night, the same dance shows up around intimacy. One partner initiates. The other feels pressure and puts the brakes on. One feels rejected, the other feels pushed.

And the two cycles feed each other. Feeling distant in the day makes it hard to open up at night. And when intimacy keeps going wrong, the distance grows.

The way out starts with naming the cycle together, so it becomes the problem instead of each other.

I'm Tarisha, a relationship and sex therapist in Auckland, trained in Emotionally Focused Therapy. I help couples with both cycles, together.

Book a free discovery session. Auckland or online."
