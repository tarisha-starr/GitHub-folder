# Two Weeks of Lunchboxes + Shopping Lists (Teen Edition, 12 to 18)

Built from two things: what the "Lunchbox Kids" page (lunchboxsystem.online) actually pictures, and what dietitians and parents online say teenagers will eat. No purchase needed: every item they show is a standard recipe, written out below.

---

## Part 1: What teens 12 to 18 actually eat

Pulled from dietitian guides, parent blogs and school nutrition pages. The sources agree on more than you'd expect.

**The rules that come up again and again**

1. **Ten minutes to eat.** Teens often have about ten minutes between the bell and their friends. Food that needs assembling, peeling, mixing or many lids comes home untouched. One-handed food wins.
2. **Not babyish.** If it looks like a little kid's bento next to their mates' food, it gets binned or traded. Deconstructed "bowls" and subs read as cool. Cute shapes don't.
3. **No soggy, no smelly.** Wilted sandwiches, warm yoghurt and anything that smells by third period get rejected. Pack wet things separately, toast the wrap, use an ice pack.
4. **Familiar plus one treat.** Include at least one thing they reliably eat and one small fun thing (a cookie, chips). The treat is what makes them open the box.
5. **Repetition is fine.** Teens prefer the same five things on rotation more than variety. Don't fight it.
6. **Let them pick from a short list.** The biggest lever in every dietitian source. Give them three mains to choose from on Sunday, they pack it themselves the night before, they eat it.
7. **Several small items beat one big one.** Bento style gets more food into them than a single sandwich.
8. **Protein per lunch:** one parent-of-teens source says at least 85g of actual protein food per lunch. The NZ and Australian Nutrient Reference Values put the daily protein intake at about 65g for boys 14 to 18 and 45g for girls 14 to 18, a touch less at 12 to 13. Lunch should carry roughly a third of that, so **aim for 20 to 30g of protein in the box.** Teens also need more protein per kilo than adults because they're growing, more again if they train.

## Week 1

### Sunday batch cook (about 90 minutes)

Oven on at 200°C. Start the oven items first, do the stovetop while they bake.

1. **Chicken nuggets** (main)
   Cut 1kg boneless chicken thigh into bite-size pieces. Three bowls: flour with salt and paprika, 2 beaten eggs, panko with a handful of grated parmesan. Coat, lay on a lined tray, spray with oil, bake 20 to 25 minutes, flipping once. Half in the fridge, half in the freezer.

2. **Ham & cheese sticks** (main), makes 12
   Cut the crusts off 12 slices of sandwich bread and roll each one flat with a rolling pin. Lay a slice of ham and a finger of cheese (or a string cheese) on each, roll up tight. Dip in beaten egg, roll in breadcrumbs, seam-down on a lined tray. Spray with oil, bake 12 to 15 minutes until golden. Great cold.

3. **Taco mince + rice** (main)
   Brown 500g beef mince, stir in a packet of taco seasoning and a splash of water, simmer 5 minutes. Cook 2 cups of rice (or use microwave pouches on the day). Fridge both.

4. **Banana muffins** (baked), makes 12
   Mash 3 very ripe bananas. Mix in 2 eggs, ½ cup Greek yoghurt, ⅓ cup honey, 1 tsp vanilla. Fold in 1 cup wholemeal flour, 1 cup plain flour, 1 tsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda, 1 tsp cinnamon. Top each with a slice of banana. Bake 18 to 20 minutes at 180°C. Freeze half.

5. **Oat cookies** (sweet), makes about 20
   Melt 125g butter with 2 tbsp golden syrup. Mix 1 cup oats, 1 cup flour, ¾ cup desiccated coconut, ½ cup brown sugar. Dissolve 1 tsp baking soda in 2 tbsp boiling water, add to the butter, pour over the dry mix. Roll balls, flatten, bake 12 minutes at 170°C.

6. **Energy truffles** (sweet), makes about 20
   Blitz 1½ cups pitted dates, 1 cup oats, ⅓ cup peanut butter, 2 tbsp cocoa, pinch of salt. Roll into balls, roll in coconut. Fridge. Nut-free school? Sunflower seed butter.

7. **Sides prep:** carrot and cucumber sticks in a container with damp paper towel, cherry tomatoes rinsed, grapes washed, cheese cut into cubes, 8 eggs boiled.

## Week 2

### Sunday batch cook (about 90 minutes)

Oven on at 200°C.

1. **Cheesy meatball bites** (main), makes about 30
   Mix 1kg beef mince, 1 egg, ½ cup breadcrumbs, 2 tbsp grated parmesan, 1 tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp dried oregano, salt and pepper. Take a walnut-size lump, push a cube of mozzarella into the middle, seal it in. Roll in beaten egg then breadcrumbs. Bake on a lined tray 18 to 20 minutes. Freeze half. (Grate a carrot into the mince if you want. It disappears.)

2. **Shredded chicken** (main)
   Buy a hot rotisserie chicken. Shred the lot while it's warm. Half gets tossed with 2 tbsp teriyaki sauce, half stays plain for Caesar wraps. Cook 2 cups rice. Fridge.

3. **Ham & cheese pastry cups** (main), makes 12
   The page's version is spinach and cheese. This is the same thing with ham for kids who won't touch green. Cut 2 sheets of thawed puff pastry into 12 squares. Press each into a greased muffin tin so the corners stick up. Whisk 4 eggs with ¼ cup milk, salt and pepper. Into each cup: chopped ham, a pinch of grated cheese, then egg to three-quarters full. Bake 18 to 20 minutes until puffed and golden.

4. **Cheese scones** (baked), makes 12
   Rub 60g cold butter into 2 cups flour with 3 tsp baking powder and ½ tsp salt. Stir in 1½ cups grated tasty cheese. Add ¾ cup milk, bring together gently, pat out 3cm thick, cut squares. Brush tops with milk. Bake 12 to 15 minutes at 220°C. Freeze half.

5. **Carrot loaf** (sweet), 1 loaf
   Whisk 2 eggs, ½ cup oil, ½ cup brown sugar, 1 tsp vanilla. Stir in 2 cups grated carrot. Fold in 1½ cups flour, 1 tsp baking powder, ½ tsp baking soda, 2 tsp cinnamon, pinch of salt. Loaf tin, 45 to 50 minutes at 170°C. Drizzle with ½ cup icing sugar mixed with a squeeze of lemon once cool.

6. **Mini pancakes**, makes about 24
   1½ cups flour, 1 tbsp sugar, 2 tsp baking powder, pinch salt, 1 egg, 1¼ cups milk, 2 tbsp melted butter. Spoon small rounds into a pan, flip when bubbles pop. Cool, stack with baking paper between, freeze in a bag. Pack frozen, they thaw by lunch. Doubles as breakfast.

7. **Sides prep:** carrot and cucumber sticks, cherry tomatoes, grapes, cheese cubes, 8 boiled eggs.

### Monday to Friday

| Day | Main | Crunch | Veg | Fruit | Sweet / Baked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Meatball sub: 5 bites, cheese, tomato sauce in a long roll | Crackers | Carrot sticks | Grapes | Cheese scone |
| Tue | Teriyaki chicken rice bowl with edamame, teriyaki pot on the side | Rice crackers | Cucumber sticks | Apple | Carrot loaf slice |
| Wed | Pastry cups x3 + Greek yoghurt pouch | Popcorn | Cherry tomatoes | Mandarin | Mini pancakes + choc chip pot |
| Thu | Chicken Caesar wrap, toasted, in foil | Corn chips | Carrot sticks + hummus | Kiwifruit, halved, with a spoon | Carrot loaf slice |
| Fri | Pizza box: pita, salami, mozzarella cubes, pizza sauce pot, boiled egg | It's in the box | Whatever veg is left | Pear or grapes | Cheese scone + truffles from the freezer |

**Night before:** Tuesday's bowl goes together cold (rice, chicken, frozen edamame straight in, it thaws). Thursday's wrap is toasted and foiled. Monday's sub can go in warm in a thermos-style food jar, or cold.

**The thermos option:** for a kid who wants hot food, preheat a food jar with boiling water for 5 minutes, tip it out, fill with hot meatballs and sauce, or mac and cheese with ham. It stays warm 4 to 6 hours.

## Making it stick with teenagers

- **Sunday, show them the list.** Three mains to choose from for the week. They pick, you cook. A lunch they chose gets eaten.
- **They pack it, the night before.** Line up one fridge shelf: mains, baking, cut veg, dips. The page sells this as a "fridge station" upsell. It's a shelf and a rule.
- **Keep the treat.** The cookie or the corn chips is why the box gets opened at all.
- **Freezer does Week 2 for you.** Half of everything baked goes in on Sunday, so Week 2's nuggets and Week 1's muffins are already done.
- **Rotate formats, not recipes.** Wraps one week, bowls the next, subs after that. Same five proteins underneath.
- **Swap freely.** Every main here is either "protein wrapped in crumbs or pastry" or "protein in a wrap, roll or bowl". Swap the filling, keep the method.
