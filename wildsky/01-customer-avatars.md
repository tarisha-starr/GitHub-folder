# Wild Sky Coffee — Customer Avatars

Built from the current site (wildskycoffee.com), the product range, the pricing,
and the NZ/AU shipping footprint. Where something is an assumption rather than
something the site told me, it is marked **[assumption — check against your
Shopify and Instagram data]**.

The point of this document is not to admire the customer. It is to decide what
the site has to say, in what order, so she stops bouncing.

---

## The one-line version

> Wild Sky sells titanium objects. Your customer is buying a feeling — the
> twenty minutes at the top before anyone else is awake — but she **decides**
> with grams, millilitres and "will this fit in my pack".
>
> The site currently does the feeling beautifully and the deciding almost not at
> all. That gap is the whole conversion problem.

## Avatar 1 — Nic, the gram-counting tramper (PRIMARY)

**Who** 42, works in IT in Wellington, does three or four multi-day trips a
year plus a Great Walk most summers. Owns a Jetboil, a Macpac pack she's had for
nine years, and a drawer of gear she regrets buying.

**What she's actually buying** Permission to carry a luxury. She has already
decided coffee is worth the weight. She wants someone to hand her the lightest
possible way to do it so she doesn't have to feel silly about it.

**Goal** A proper coffee on day three without adding meaningful weight or a
single extra decision.

**Biggest fear** Carrying something up a hill and discovering it was a waste of
space. Second fear: that this is a pretty brand selling rebadged gear she could
get for half the price.

**Current solution** Instant sachets she resents, or a plastic Aeropress that
stains and smells. A steel mug that is heavy and burns her lip.

**How she decides** In this order: weight in grams → millilitres → does it nest
with what I own → price. She will open three tabs and compare. She wants the
spec before the story.

**Her objections, in her words**
- "$59 for a cup?"
- "Single wall or double wall — which one do I actually want?"
- "Will the mug sit on my stove? Can I boil in it?"
- "Does the press fit inside the pot?"
- "85g versus 45g — what am I getting for the extra 40 grams?"
- "Titanium, sure, but who made it?"

**What tips her over** A weight number next to every product, a comparison she
doesn't have to build herself, and one sentence that proves a human carried this
up a hill in the Kaimanawas.

**What she'd type into Google**
`titanium camping mug nz` · `lightweight coffee press tramping` ·
`ultralight coffee setup nz` · `titanium mug vs steel` ·
`best coffee for multi day hike new zealand`

**Words that work on her** grams, nests, earns its place, single wall, 220ml,
packs down, buy once. **Words that lose her** "elevate", "curated",
"premium lifestyle", anything with an exclamation mark.

**Likely order** Summit Mug + Scooper + Bean Vault, or the press on its own.
Average order $40–$90.

## Avatar 2 — Sarah, the slow-morning adventurer (PRIMARY, highest value)

**Who** 51, Auckland, two teenagers, a demanding job and a hilux or a van.
Weekends away, day walks, a kayak on the roof, a summer at Tarawera. Not chasing
summits — chasing the bit where nobody needs anything from her.

**What she's actually buying** The ritual. The twenty minutes with a cup in both
hands before the day starts asking. The gear is the excuse to take the twenty
minutes.

**Goal** To make the mornings away feel like something, not like camping
logistics.

**Biggest fear** That it ends up as more stuff in the boot that never gets used.
She has bought beautiful things before and left them in a cupboard.

**Current solution** A travel mug from the servo, a plunger that lives in the
van and leaks, instant when she can't be bothered.

**How she decides** She decides on feel and then looks for a reason it is
sensible. Photography matters enormously. She will buy the whole setup at once
if someone shows her what the whole setup is.

**Her objections, in her words**
- "Do I need the table, or am I being ridiculous?"
- "Is this going to be one more thing to wash and cart around?"
- "Which of these do I actually need to start?"
- "Is it easy? I don't want a ceremony with eleven steps."

**What tips her over** A photographed, named, priced kit — "here are the four
things, $148, this is the morning it makes". She does not want to assemble a
cart from ten loose objects. She wants the Brew Table moment, and right now the
site describes it better than it sells it.

**What she'd type into Google**
`camping coffee setup` · `nice camp coffee gear nz` ·
`van life coffee kit` · `camping gift for coffee lover`

**Words that work on her** slow morning, both hands, no rush, take your time,
the good stuff, beautifully made. **Words that lose her** bivvy, grams-per-litre,
anything that sounds like a spec sheet shouting.

**Likely order** Brew Table + Kea Cup, or a full kit. Average order $120–$250.
She is the reason bundles matter.

## Avatar 3 — Mark's mate's partner, the gift buyer (SECONDARY, currently unserved)

**Who** 38–65, buying for a partner, dad or adult kid who is "into hiking".
Knows nothing about titanium and is slightly afraid of getting it wrong.

**Goal** One good present that lands, bought in under ten minutes, arriving
before Christmas.

**Biggest fear** Buying the wrong thing, or a parcel that shows up on 27
December.

**How she decides** Price band first ("something around $50"), then "does it
look like a gift", then delivery date. She will not read a spec table.

**Her objections, in her words**
- "Will he already have one?"
- "What if it's the wrong size?"
- "Will it get here in time?"
- "Can I just get the lot?"

**What tips her over** Gift-by-price navigation ("under $30 / around $60 / the
full kit"), a visible last-order date for Christmas, and a returns line she can
see without hunting in the footer.

**What she'd type into Google**
`gift for someone who loves hiking nz` · `gift for coffee lover under $50 nz` ·
`stocking stuffers for trampers`

**Likely order** One item at $28–$65, or a bundle if you build one. She is the
single fastest revenue win on this list because the site currently says nothing
to her at all. It is mid-September — the Christmas window is open now.

## Avatar 4 — the coffee person who happens to camp (SECONDARY, highest ticket)

**Who** 30–50, has opinions about grind size, follows a local roaster, owns an
Aeropress and possibly a scale. The $168 Timemore grinder is for exactly this
person and nobody else on this list.

**Goal** Not to drink worse coffee just because they left the house.

**Biggest fear** Compromise. Bad extraction, stale pre-ground, a brew method
that can't be dialled in.

**Her objections, in her words**
- "What grind does the press want?"
- "How much coffee per cup, and at what ratio?"
- "Why would I carry a 550g grinder?"
- "Is this just camping gear with coffee written on it?"

**What tips her over** A brew guide. Dose, grind, water, time, for the Traverse
press specifically. That one page earns search traffic, earns trust with this
segment, and gives everyone else a reason to come back.

**Likely order** Grinder + press + cup. Average order $250+.

## Audience profile and segments (for ad targeting and content)

**Core profile** 30–60, NZ-based with an AU tail, household income comfortable
enough for discretionary gear, outdoors as identity rather than occasional
hobby. Skews slightly female in the ritual segment and slightly male in the
ultralight segment **[assumption — check your Shopify customer data and
Instagram audience breakdown]**. Buys seasonally: Labour Weekend to Easter is
the NZ tramping season, with a hard gift spike in late November and December.

| Segment | Size of opportunity | Messaging angle | Where they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gram-counting trampers | Steady, lower AOV | Weight, nesting, buy once | NZ tramping Facebook groups, Reddit r/trampingNZ, YouTube gear reviews, Google search |
| Slow-morning adventurers | Largest AOV | The twenty minutes, the setup, the ritual | Instagram and TikTok (your existing content is built for them), Pinterest |
| Gift buyers | Seasonal spike, fast win | "One good present for someone who loves getting outside" | Google search Nov–Dec, Meta retargeting, email |
| Coffee people outdoors | Smallest, highest ticket | Brew quality, dialling it in, the grinder | Specialty coffee content, roaster collabs, brew guides |

**Channel and timing recommendations**

1. **Instagram and TikTok are already right for Sarah, not for Nic.** The
   adventure reels sell the feeling. Add a second content line — short, plain
   "what's in the kit, what it weighs" clips — or the tramper never converts
   from social.
2. **Google is where Nic and the gift buyer live, and the site is currently
   invisible there** — the homepage title tag is literally just "Wild Sky
   Coffee". See `05-seo-pack.md`; this is the cheapest traffic on the table.
3. **Post the morning, not the product, at 6–8am NZT.** Both primary avatars
   are thinking about coffee at that hour. Product shots convert better in the
   evening scroll.
4. **Email is the asset you don't have yet.** The 20% popup collects addresses
   into a Shopify list with no sequence behind it. A five-email welcome
   sequence, written once, will outsell another month of posting. See
   `06-offer-bundles-email.md`.
5. **One roaster collaboration** would do more for credibility with segments 2
   and 4 than any amount of paid social: a NZ roaster's beans in a Bean Vault,
   both audiences see it, neither of you discounts anything.

## The three things every page has to answer

Every avatar above bounces for one of three reasons. Fix these and the rest of
the work is decoration.

1. **"Which one do I buy?"** Three cups, no comparison. Nobody should have to
   open three tabs to choose between a 45g mug, a 70g cup and an 85g cup.
2. **"Will it work with my stuff?"** Dimensions, nesting, whether it can go on
   a flame. Not in the description — in a table, at decision height.
3. **"Can I trust you with $59?"** No reviews, no guarantee near the button, no
   shipping promise where she's deciding, and free NZ shipping hidden two clicks
   away in a policy page.
