## Part 1 — What I found in the Notion hub, and one problem worth fixing today

The **Wild Sky Coffee — Content Hub** is a serious piece of work: 100 reel ideas
in nine buckets, a shoot guide, 100 quotes, a knowledge base with ratios and
grind sizes. The pipeline isn't the bottleneck. Three things in it are wrong,
though, and they'll leak into every reel shot from it:

**1. The product table is wrong on nearly every price.**

| Product | Notion says | Site says |
|---|---|---|
| Traverse Coffee Press | $30 | **$65** |
| Kea Mountain Cup | $80 | **$59** |
| Summit Mug | $80 | **$25** |
| Camp Cup | $80 | **$35** |
| Trail Spork | $30 | **$14** |
| Wild Coffee Scooper | $30 | **$16** |
| Brew Table | $80 | **$84** |
| Alpine Pot | $80 | **$79** |
| Bean Vault | $30 | **$28** |
| **First Light Pour Over** | **$3** | **doesn't exist** |

Eleven products listed, ten on the site. "First Light Pour Over" has a whole
hero reel written for it (*"$3 of gear. A million dollar view."*) and a tutorial
bucket built around pour-over technique — for a product you don't sell. Fix the
table before anyone shoots from it, or delete the pour-over reels.

**2. The voice in Notion isn't the voice on your site.** Notion says "poetic,
sensory, contemplative… never salesy… end on silence, not a hard CTA." Your site
says *"transforms tired grumpy hikers into perfectly nice human beings."*

The site voice is better and it is more yours: dry, understated, Kiwi, funny.
Poetic-contemplative is the single most crowded lane in outdoor content —
every brand with a drone and a sunrise is in it. The dry humour is the thing
almost nobody in titanium gear is doing. **Pick the site voice and let Notion
follow it**, not the other way round.

**3. "Never a hard CTA" is costing you money.** Ritual content with no ask
builds an audience that never visits the site. You don't need to be salesy —
but "link in bio, weights on every product" at the end of a gear reel is not
salesy, it's useful.

## Part 2 — Outliers: what actually travels in this niche

I can't hand you a 50-row outlier sheet with honest multipliers, because the
view counts aren't publicly readable. What I can give you is the format-level
picture, and it's clear enough to act on.

**The competitive benchmark, and it's the most useful number here.**
[SilverAnt Outdoors](https://www.instagram.com/silverantoutdoors/) — the closest
direct competitor, ultralight titanium gear including a titanium French press and
a double-wall coffee cup — has roughly **18,000 followers off about 4,000 posts.**

Sit with that. Four thousand posts. That's the ceiling on "post product photos of
titanium consistently and hope". Volume is not the strategy in this category, and
if you copy the category you get the category's results.

**What the niche rewards** ([outdoor trend coverage for 2026](https://pbfulfill.com/blogs/marketing-strategy/most-viral-outdoor-products)
documents the camp-coffee ritual as its own content category now, with
#CampCoffeeChallenge and #MorningInTheWild as live tags):

| Format | Why it travels | Are you doing it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunrise ritual, minimal cuts, real audio** | The emotional core of the category. Saves and shares, not comments. | Yes — it's your instinct already |
| **ASMR brew sequences** — grind, click, pour, hiss, plunge | Fastest-growing outdoor format; watch-time engine. Weather as a base layer, 3–5 distinct triggers per scene | Not systematically |
| **Weighed kit breakdowns** — every item, every gram, the total | The single format that converts trampers, and almost nobody shoots it properly | No |
| **Comparison and tests** — heat retention, boil race, pack weight | Comment-bait that's also genuinely useful. Arguments in the comments are the point | No |
| **Comedy tension** — the friend balancing a pot on a rock | Broadest reach of anything here, and it's already written into your Brew Table copy | No |

**The gap you can own.** The category is saturated with reverence — misty
sunrises, slow pours, quiet piano. It is almost empty of **specificity** (grams,
millilitres, side-by-side tests) and almost empty of **humour**. You have both
sitting in your product descriptions already. That's the wedge.

**The rule to work to:** a reel under ~500 views in 24 hours means the hook
failed, not the content. Refilm the first two seconds and repost — that's your
own working rule from the Radiant Woman reels doc, and it applies here.

## Part 3 — 30 angles to promote the brand

Not 30 more reel ideas — you have 100. These are **angles**: a position to argue
from, each with the hook to open on, who it's for, and what it sells. Cross-refs
to the avatars in `01-customer-avatars.md`.

### A. Proof and specificity — for Nic, the gram-counter (1–6)

**1. Every gram, on the scale, on camera.**
Hook: *"Two days of coffee. Here's every gram of it."*
Kitchen scale, one item at a time, running total on screen. Ends on the number.
Sells the Summit Kit. Nobody in this category shoots this honestly.

**2. The 40 grams question.**
Hook: *"45 grams or 85. What does the extra 40 buy you?"*
Summit Mug vs Kea Cup, side by side, two hands around each. Sells both cups by
making the choice easy instead of hiding it.

**3. The boil race.**
Hook: *"Alpine Pot versus the Jetboil. One of these is 79 grams."*
Split screen, timer running. You will lose on speed and win on weight — say so.
Losing honestly on one axis is what makes the other claim believable.

**4. Heat retention, measured.**
Hook: *"Double wall, single wall, and a thermometer."*
Three cups, same water, temperature at 5, 10, 20 minutes. Turns a $34 price gap
into a number.

**5. What fits inside the pot.**
Hook: *"Everything in this pack fits inside this pot."*
One continuous shot, items going in, lid on. **Measure it first** — this claim
must be true.
Sells the whole kit in fifteen seconds.

**6. Seven days in a Bean Vault.**
Hook: *"Same beans. One in a ziplock, one in here."*
Day 1 and day 7, the smell test on camera, faces doing the talking. Sells the
$28 item that's easiest to dismiss.

### B. The twenty minutes — for Sarah, the slow-morning adventurer (7–12)

**7. The twenty minutes before anyone's awake.**
Hook: *"Nobody needs anything from me for the next twenty minutes."*
Your whole brand thesis in one line. Real audio, no music, one cut. This is the
one to pin to your profile.

**8. The rock versus the table.**
Hook: *"Your mate's setup. Your setup."*
Straight from your own Brew Table copy — the pot wobbling on a rock, then the
Chabudai. Comedy and product demo in one, and it's the best writing on your site.

**9. Coffee in both hands.**
Hook: *"This is why there are no handles."*
Close on the Kea Cup, two hands, cold morning, steam. Sells the most expensive
cup on feel, which is how it gets bought.

**10. The morning you keep going back to.**
Hook: *"One morning I think about more than I should."*
A named place — Tarawera, the Kaimanawas — one brew, one sit. Location tag. Place
tags are the discovery engine in outdoor content.

**11. Making it for someone else.**
Hook: *"Handing someone a coffee is the smallest love language there is."*
Two cups, one handed over. Sells the press (makes two) and the pair of cups.

**12. The slow bit is the point.**
Hook: *"The coffee takes four minutes. That's the whole reason."*
Grind, bloom, plunge, sit. Not a tutorial — an argument for taking the time.

### C. Comedy and tension — broadest reach (13–17)

**13. Tired grumpy hikers.**
Hook: *"Before coffee. After coffee."*
Your own line, acted out. Two shots. Do it with actual friends and actual faces.

**14. The gear-shaming bit.**
Hook: *"He said the coffee kit was a luxury. Then he drank it."*
The person who mocked the extra 200g on day one and is first in the queue on
day three. Every tramping group has this person.

**15. Things in my pack that just make sense.**
Hook: *"Four things in my pack that make no sense to anyone else."*
Trend format, your kit, deadpan delivery.

**16. POV: you forgot the grinder.**
Hook: *"Two hours in. No grinder."*
The rock-and-bag improvisation, played straight, ending in a grim sip. Comedy
that also sells the grinder.

**17. Rate our worst camp coffee.**
Hook: *"Show us your worst. We'll go first."*
Duet and comment bait. You go first with a genuinely terrible one — the
vulnerability is what makes people reply.

### D. Place and New Zealand identity (18–21)

**18. Brewed in places, named for places.**
Hook: *"The Kea Cup is named after a bird that would steal it."*
The kea story — smartest bird in the world, notorious thief. It is the best
product name you have and the story isn't being told.

**19. Coffee at the end of every Great Walk.**
Hook: *"Ten walks. Ten brews. One cup."*
A running series with a location tag each time. Builds a body of work, and the
tags do the distribution.

**20. Designed here, tested here, and here's the proof.**
Hook: *"This is where we test it."*
Not a studio. Mud, wind, a hut. The antidote to the drop-shipped titanium brands
you're competing with on a search page.

**21. The alpine water argument.**
Hook: *"Does mountain water actually make better coffee?"*
Stream water vs bottled, same beans, blind taste with a mate. Genuinely
interesting, genuinely arguable, and the comments do the work.

### E. Gift season — for the gift buyer, publish from late October (22–25)

**22. One good present for someone who's into hiking.**
Hook: *"They already own everything. Except this."*
Three price bands in thirty seconds. This is your single biggest seasonal angle
and the site currently says nothing to this buyer.

**23. Under $30, and it gets used every single day.**
Hook: *"The cheapest thing we make is the one they'll use most."*
Scooper and spork. Stocking-filler positioning at a price nobody argues with.

**24. The Christmas cut-off, said out loud.**
Hook: *"Australia, you've got until [date]. New Zealand, [date]."*
Honest urgency, hard dates, no countdown theatre. Post it twice.

**25. Don't buy him another head torch.**
Hook: *"He's got four head torches."*
Comedy built on a true observation about gift-buying for outdoors people.

### F. Distribution beyond your own audience (26–30)

**26. One roaster, one Bean Vault.**
A NZ roaster's beans in your vault, both audiences see it, nobody discounts
anything. The highest-leverage partnership available to you, and it lends you
the credibility of an established coffee name.

**27. The retail demo.**
Bivouac or a local outdoor shop, brewing at the counter on a Saturday. Content,
retail conversation and a footpath full of exactly your customer, all at once.

**28. Two ambassadors, not twenty.**
A hut warden and a trail runner. Give them the kit, ask for nothing, let them
use it for a season. Guides and wardens are trusted in a way influencers aren't.

**29. #WildSkyMornings, and actually run it.**
Repost every single one. A monthly winner gets a Kea Cup. UGC is the only content
that scales past what the two of you can shoot, and it solves the zero-reviews
problem at the same time — ask each poster for two sentences for the site.

**30. Turn every reel into an email.**
The weighed-kit reel is email four in the welcome sequence. The cup comparison is
email two. The brew method is email three. You're already making the content —
sending it to a list you own is the difference between renting an audience and
having one.

## Part 4 — Fix the profile before you post another thing

Twenty minutes of work, and it converts everything that follows:

- **Bio** — say what you sell and where you ship. *"Titanium coffee gear for the
  outdoors · Designed and tested in NZ · Free NZ shipping"* beats a philosophy.
- **Link** — straight to the Coffee Gear collection, not the homepage. Once the
  Gifts page exists, switch it in November.
- **Pin three reels** — the twenty minutes (7), the weighed kit (1), the rock
  versus the table (8). One emotional, one useful, one funny.
- **Highlights** — Which cup? · What's in the kit · Where we've brewed · Reviews.
- **Location tag every single post.** In outdoor niches the place tag is the
  discovery engine, and it's free.
- **Product tags on every reel** — Instagram Shopping against your Shopify
  catalogue. You can't buy from a reel that isn't tagged.

## Part 5 — A posting rhythm you can actually keep

Three a week, not daily. Two people running a business can't sustain daily, and
the category punishes filler more than it punishes silence.

- **One proof reel** (group A) — the format nobody else shoots
- **One ritual reel** (group B) — the brand
- **One comedy or comment-bait reel** (group C) — the reach

Shoot a month in a day. One trip, one kit, fifteen clips. Post at 6–8am NZT for
ritual content and evening for product. Then:

**Check one number monthly:** profile visits → link clicks. Followers are
vanity here; a small account that sends 200 qualified people to a product page
beats 18,000 followers that send nobody.
