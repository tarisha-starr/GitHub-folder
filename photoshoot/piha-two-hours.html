Before you start

Forty shots in an hour is ninety seconds a shot

It is a fast hour, and it only works because the shots come in tens without moving. Get to a station, shoot ten, walk to the next. No wandering, no reviewing on the back of the camera between frames, no discussion about what to do next. That is the whole trick.

Where each outfit earns its place

The flowing dress goes in the wind and the water, because movement is the only thing that dress does better than the others, and Piha supplies wind for free. The cocktail dress goes on the black rock, where the contrast is sharpest. Jeans carry everything warm and human, the thermos, the blanket, the laughing. The skirt and top go on Mark's hour at Lion Rock, where the frames have to sit next to a retreat price and be believed.

The cocktail dress will be freezing, and it is worth it

Twelve minutes on wet black rock in a cocktail dress in August is genuinely cold. Coat on between every take, someone holding it ready, and shoot that station faster than the others. It is the most striking set on either list: a cocktail dress on black iron sand looks like nothing else in your niche, and it gives you the press and podcast frames in one go.

Shoot it in the last two hours of daylight if you can choose

Every frame here works in flat midday light and about fifteen of them get much better in low sun: the backlit ones, the rim lit hair, the long shadows, anything through spray haze. If the timing is yours to pick, start hour one two hours before sunset and let hour two finish in the last of it.

Bring these or the hour stalls

A coat for between takes, a wool blanket that doubles as a prop, a thermos, dry socks, a bin liner to change on, a comb and a soft brush for sand in your hair. Iron sand is abrasive, so no lens changes on the beach. Black sand also fools the meter, so dial in about a stop under and expose for skin.

If an hour gets cut short

The ten worth losing everything else for

Weather, cold, a late start. Get these and both sites can still be built.

  • S1 standing into the wind, and S33 backlit close through the haze. One is the course, the other is the portrait everything else hangs off.
  • S3 hands back through your hair. The whole reason for the trip.
  • S30 full length, straight to lens, feet apart. The frame that needs no caption.
  • S37 blanket round the shoulders, straight to lens. It sells the opt in on its own.
  • D11 walking at camera, mid conversation. Most likely homepage hero.
  • D1 shoulder to shoulder, serious. The frame that has to sit next to a retreat price.
  • D22 and D23 the gap, then the hands found, same locked off frame. Two pictures that say what your work does.
  • D31 and D32 the turn away and the repair. The same idea again, and the pair the therapy page is built on.

Rain is not a reason to cancel. Piha in rain gives wet black rock, low cloud on the ridge and an empty beach, and the rock station and the car boot station both work better in it. Only wind over about 40 knots or a big swell is worth moving the date for. Nobody goes past ankle depth at any point, the water there is the most dangerous in the country.
