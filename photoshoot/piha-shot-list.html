Turn your back on the sea for the pretty light

Between ten and twelve the sun is in the northeast and climbing, so Piha is lit from behind the land. Facing the water means flat frontal light on faces. Every glowing backlit frame on this list needs you to turn around and shoot inland, toward Lion Rock and the bush, with the sun up beyond it. That is the opposite of what feels right on a beach, so decide it now rather than on the sand.

What two hours cannot buy, and how to get it

Everything built on low sun is gone today: S33 at its best, S35 to S39, S38, S40, D31, D35 to D40. That includes D37, the signature couple frame, and S39, the strongest portrait on the list. They need one more trip, and only a short one. Forty five minutes at Piha starting at 5.10pm on any clear evening picks up all ten. Same clothes, same beach, one carpark.

Skipped on purpose, so nobody wonders

The Kitekite falls track is thirty five minutes each way and would eat the entire window, so block 1 is out. North Piha lagoon is a separate drive, so block 2 moves to the waterline in front of the main carpark instead. Both are worth doing on the return trip if you want them.

Move fast and cut in this order

If you are running behind, the tailgate block is the one to shorten, because it can happen anywhere on any day. Protect the dunes block and the waterline block. If you only get through two of the five, make them those two.

The direction

Piha gives us Pine Deep for free

Your brand anchor is Pine Deep and the rule is that cream acts as relief, not the other way round. Piha does that for us in the landscape. The iron sand, the wet rock, the water under cloud: all of it sits in that dark green black range without any help. So we do not have to build the mood. We only have to put the warm thing in the frame.

That warm thing is you. Cream, oatmeal, camel, rust and terracotta all sing off black sand in a way they never do off a studio backdrop, and terracotta stays exactly where the guidelines want it, an accent inside a dark field rather than a wash. Every frame in this list is built the same way: dark ground, warm skin, one lit person, wide horizon behind.

The haircut is the reason to shoot here now. Piha is windy nearly every day of the year, and long hair on that beach means a whole day of fighting it. Short hair reads the opposite way. Wind gives it shape, movement and edge light, so we shoot straight into it rather than sheltering from it. The cut appears in every block, in every light, in profile, in the turn, wet, wind pushed and still.

What we are not doing: no posed studio, no white backgrounds, no arms folded in a doorway, and on the couple frames, none of the "look how happy we are" stock posture. The couple shots that carry the site are the quiet ones. Backs turned, looking out. A hand found. A shared look across a thermos. Two people who have been together long enough that the closeness is in the shoulders, not the smile.

Before the day

The eight things that decide whether this works

Most of these are booked or packed before anyone picks up a camera. The tide one changes the date, so settle it first.

Pick the date off the tide, not the diary

Book a day where low tide falls between about 3pm and 5.30pm. That is the only way the mirror sand and the long light land together, and half the strongest frames in this list need both. Check the tide tables for Manukau Heads, which is the reference port for Piha. If the tide is wrong, move the date.

Piha water is the most dangerous in the country

Nobody goes past ankle depth, including for the sunset frames. Do not put anyone or a camera bag on the Taitomo rocks or near The Gap if there is any swell running. Check the surf forecast the night before. If it is big, we lose two frames and keep everyone, which is a fine trade.

Late winter light means no dead hour

The sun barely gets above 35 degrees in Auckland at this time of year, so there is no harsh midday to hide from. That is why this plan works straight through from 7am with only a short break. Sunset is around 5.45pm, and the carpark goes properly dark fast afterwards, so pack the torch.

Black sand lies to the light meter

A frame full of iron sand pushes the meter to overexpose, and you get grey mush where the sand should be rich. Dial in two thirds to one and a third of a stop under, expose for her skin, and let the sand sit dark. Read the histogram, not the screen. On a bright beach the screen will tell you it is fine when it is a stop hot.

Iron sand is magnetic and it is abrasive

No lens changes anywhere on the sand. Two bodies if you have them, one long and one wide, and tape the seams. Sea spray gives free diffusion when shooting into the sun, which is a gift, but check the front element every ten frames and keep the cloth in a zip bag so it stays dry.

Wardrobe: cream and rust, never head to toe black

Cream, oatmeal, camel, rust, terracotta and pine green all separate beautifully from black sand. Head to toe black sinks into it, so keep black for the deliberate silhouettes in block seven. No pure white, it clips before the skin does. No logos and no fine busy prints, they moire. Fabric that moves is worth more than fabric that fits.

A kit list that is mostly not camera kit

Wool blanket that doubles as a prop, thermos, two towels, spare socks, a bin liner to sit on, a fine brush for sand in hair, a comb, texture paste, lip balm, a torch, and dry clothes for the drive home. Cold and wind will end the day early if nobody plans for them, and the last two blocks are the best two.

Ask for the crops up front

Eighty finals graded warm, plus flat ungraded versions of the ten hero frames so the site build can crop and colour without fighting a bake. Every frame flagged for social needs 4:5 and 9:16 as well as the master. And at least twelve frames with deliberate empty space on one side, because headlines have to sit somewhere.

If the day goes sideways

The ten frames worth losing everything else for

Rain, a blown out forecast, a shorter day than planned. Shoot these and the sites still get built.

  • S18 standing into the wind, and S33 backlit close through the haze. One is the course, the other is the portrait everything else hangs off.
  • S39 the last light on your face. The single strongest solo portrait if the sunset shows up.
  • S21 hands through the cut. The whole reason for the day.
  • S29 blanket round the shoulders, straight to lens. It sells the opt in on its own.
  • D29 the walk toward camera. Most likely homepage hero.
  • D37 foreheads together with the sun burning between you. The signature couple frame.
  • D9 and D10 the gap and the hands found, same locked off frame. Two pictures that say what your work does.
  • D14 shoulder to shoulder, straight to lens, serious. The frame that has to sit next to a retreat price.

Rain is not a reason to cancel. Piha in rain gives wet black rock, low cloud on the ridge and an empty beach, and blocks 1, 3 and 5 all work better in it. Only wind over about 40 knots or a big swell is worth moving the date for.
