Three pieces of kit this session cannot run without

A tripod, because four of the new frames are half second to four second exposures and nothing handheld will hold them. A torch with a warm beam, held off to the side by Mark rather than strapped to a head, and if it is a cold white LED then tape a scrap of orange gel or a sweet wrapper over it. And spare batteries, because cold plus long exposures plus live view will flatten a battery in an hour.

Get off the falls track before the light goes

Under that canopy it is properly dark well before it is dark on the beach, and the track has roots and steps. Out by 3.30 as scheduled, or carry two torches. Do not plan to come back to the falls at the end of the day.

You will get wet twice, so plan for it

The falls pool in block one and the shorebreak in block five. Two towels, two pairs of dry socks, and a change of trousers in the car. Sea is 13.5 degrees and the air goes cold fast after the sun drops, so the difference between a great blue hour and an abandoned one is dry clothes.

If you run this after 27 September

Daylight saving starts on the last Sunday in September, and sunset jumps from about 6pm to about 7.30pm. Shift the whole runsheet an hour and a half later: falls at 3.30, mirror sand at 5.15, sunset block at 6.50. The tide rule does not shift with the clock, so still pick a day with low water in the late afternoon.

Wardrobe, two changes

Bush block: cream linen and camel, the same soft palette as session one, because green wants warm against it. Beach blocks: rust or terracotta knit and cream trousers for the low sun, then pull on the dark coats for sunset and after. Head to toe dark is correct from 5.20 onward and wrong before it.

What to cut if you fall behind

The falls block, in full. It is the only one that can move to another day without losing anything, because it does not depend on the tide or the sunset. Everything from 4.45 onward is unrepeatable without another whole trip, so protect it. If the sky is flat grey at 5pm, skip the sunset frames rather than shooting them badly, and put that time into blue hour instead, which works under cloud.

If the light collapses

The eight frames this session exists for

Grey sky, a squall, a late start. Get these and the trip has paid for itself.

  • D37 foreheads together with the sun burning between your chests. The signature couple frame, and it has been outstanding since day one.
  • S39 the last orange light on your face. The strongest solo portrait available anywhere on either list.
  • S49 the hair halo. The frame that makes the haircut look like a decision.
  • D51 the kiss in silhouette. The only place on the whole list where an actual kiss works.
  • D47 two long shadows merging. No faces, and it says more about the work than most of the portraits do.
  • S53 sitting watching it go. Your closing band and your footer, in one honest frame.
  • D52 torch low between you in the blue. Warm faces in a cold world.
  • S41 still at the falls with the water gone to silk. The only frame on either list that could not be taken on a beach.

Flat grey does not kill this session, it just moves the weight. Cloud ruins the sunset block and improves the falls, the mirror sand and the blue hour, all three of which work better without hard sun. Only rain heavy enough to threaten the camera is worth turning back for.
